01

The status is the title

Open — under discussion Needs slivers · see 07
Your brief

"first I want the header to have that modern big title on the right and when I scroll the title become smaller and it becomes like the current header size … and the information in the right corner can be in the homepage, so the header returns to be dynamic"

My first board proposed "a header per page" — a title on each screen. That was too small an idea, and it missed the second half of your sentence. You are asking for two things: a large title that collapses on scroll, and the shift facts moved out of the chrome and into the page.

Glovo answers this better than I did. Their tracking screen has an almost empty top bar and makes the live status itself the big title — "Get ready", "4 minutes left", "13:30 – 13:40" — with a small state chip above it. The title is not a page name. It is the one fact the courier opened the app to read.

Applied here, the big title is «الطلب ٥ من ٨» — position in the round — with the branch demoted to an eyebrow. On scroll it shrinks to today's 53pt bar. The cash and remaining count stop being chrome and become the first row of the page, which is also what makes them free to disappear on a fresh day (section 06).

The Glovo checkout screen you sent shows the second half of this pattern, and it is worth taking as well. There, the nav bar title is small and centred — just "Checkout" — while the body carries a stack of big bold headings: "Your order", "Delivery address", "Delivery options". So "big title" is not only a collapsing nav bar; it is also section headings inside the scroll, sized like titles. That is what gives a long page its rhythm, and it is exactly what the settlement page lacks — see the ladder in section 08, where «جولات اليوم» and the returns block currently read at the same weight as body copy.

Your brief

"right now in the settlement page there is a layout of the batches in a container and expandable and collapsable so I want the same in the orders list — because the orders list have the batches floating right now"

Same collapse mechanics in both files Only the surface differs Cause: 2026-08-25, three flattening commits Batch sections shipped 2026-09-02 · 22aca9e

The cause is archaeological, not aesthetic. Three commits on 2026-08-25 flattened the app's lists — "Lists sit on the page, not on a white sheet" — and settlement's card happened to survive that pass while the queue's did not. When batch sections were built on 2026-09-02 (22aca9e) they were built on whatever foundation the queue had, which by then was the flat one. Glovo's checkout is the same shape: "Delivery options" is a bordered card holding a collapsible row, while the loose rows around it — address, gift, phone — carry no card at all. Contain what groups; leave loose rows loose. A batch is a group with a header, a count and a fold. A search result is not.

04

Unstick the settlement sub-header

Decided — it scrolls One line
Your brief

“there is like a second header sticky in the settlement so we want to make that non sticky”

Nobody wrote a sticky behaviour here. _SettlementHeader is pinned by where it sits in the tree: at settlement_screen.dart:98 it is a sibling of the Expanded that holds the SingleChildScrollView, not a child of it. A widget outside that Expanded is physically incapable of scrolling — the scroll view starts below it. So the row is not sticky by intent; it is sticky by position, and the fix is the same one line either way.

The cost is paid on the worst possible page. Settlement is the app's one long reconciliation list — cash lines, per-جولة sections, returns, then seven days of history — and it opens with two stacked fixed bars: the shell's AppHeader and this one. Together they hold roughly a fifth of the viewport for the entire scroll, repeating a branch name and a date the courier read once on arrival.

Settled direction: move _SettlementHeader inside the SingleChildScrollView, as the first child of its Column. It scrolls away with the content and the cash card gains that fifth back. This also composes with the collapsing-title work rather than competing with it: once the title collapses on scroll, this row is exactly what collapses — it cannot be both the thing that collapses and the thing that never moves.

settlement_screen.dart:98 — sibling of the Expanded, so it is outside the scrollable subtree entirely.
scrolled: true — passed as a hardcoded constant, never derived from a scroll offset. Nothing is listening.
~20% of the viewport — two permanent bars on the longest list in the app.
One line — the widget moves into the Column; no new state, no controller, no scroll listener.

Settled on the zero state: the strip is withheld until any metric moves above zero — first delivery, first failure, first cash collected. Settled on placement, with one caveat worth seeing: first on the page when there is no hero — idle, new day, returning — and under the hero once there is an active stop. Measured in your own Figma edit: moving the strip above the hero shifted the destination from about 20% to about 31% down an 874pt screen. On the hero-less states there is nothing to push, so the strip takes the top slot exactly as asked.

07

Liquid-glass tab bar

Decided No native code
Your brief "I want to include another version of the tab bar to have the same liquid glass as new iphones so can we do that? like downloading it or something and giving you the engineering directions"

Yes — and there is nothing to download. The effect itself is about eighty lines of Flutter. The real project is the layout, because a blur samples whatever is painted beneath it, and right now nothing is.

BottomNav has 12 call sites, and every one of them is the last child of a Column with Expanded(child: list) above it — app_shell.dart:311, queue_body.dart:30, orders_body.dart:51, home_screen.dart:152. The list ends at exactly the pixel where the bar begins. A grep of lib/ for extendBody|BackdropFilter|ImageFilter|CustomScrollView|SliverList returns zero hits. So a BackdropFilter dropped in today would blur a flat page fill and render indistinguishable from the opaque bar we already ship.

The layout work is therefore the spec, not a footnote: content must pass behind the bar via Scaffold(extendBody: true) or a Stack with the bar Positioned, and every scrollable gains a bottom inset of bar height + MediaQuery.viewPaddingOf(context).bottom. Skip that and the last row hides under the bar forever.

One trap to clear first: HomeIndicator paints an opaque AppColors.surface strip, and BottomNav wraps everything in a second Container(color: AppColors.surface). Both go transparent, or you get a crisp white band sitting under a blurred bar.

Road mode «وضع الطريق» forces glass off. It exists for sun on the screen and gloves on the grips; translucency is the direct enemy of legibility. This is an interlock, not a preference.
Flutter exposes no reduceTransparency flag — verified against the installed SDK: AccessibilityFeatures carries only accessibleNavigation, invertColors, disableAnimations, boldText, reduceMotion, highContrast, onOffSwitchLabels. Use MediaQuery.highContrastOf(context) as the honest proxy; the real iOS flag can later ride the app's existing LiveActivityChannel MethodChannel — no plugin, no Podfile.
When gated, follow cupertino_ui's own opaque() pattern: skip the BackdropFilter entirely rather than blurring behind an opaque fill. Paying for a save layer nobody can see is the worst of both.

Sequence

04 first. Moving one widget inside a scroll view. Smallest change on the board, and it gives the settlement page back a fifth of its height immediately.

Then 05 and 02 together. The word is 22 string keys; the container is a widget you have already written and shipped on another page. Both are low risk and both change how every later screenshot reads, so doing them first makes the rest easier to judge.

Then 03, then 06. Drop the chips once batches are contained — that ordering lets you see whether the exception row is still needed. Then the strip's placement and its zero-state rule, which is the first change that touches logic rather than layout.

01 and 07 last, and together. Both need the same migration: the body becomes the scroll owner and the chrome floats over it. Done separately you pay for it twice — a collapsing header built on a Column would need rebuilding to let content pass under a translucent bar anyway. One migration, then both are configuration. 07 is settled; 01 is not, but it does not block the migration — what the title says can be decided while the scroll architecture is being built.

The flag is not in this queue. It needs an answer, not a design.
